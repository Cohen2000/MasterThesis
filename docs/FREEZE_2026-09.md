**The retrospective answer runs opposite to the obvious guess, and that is why
the randomized split is needed.** If naming the direction carried the
disclosure effect, the two direction-naming arms would move more. They move
less: median shift 0.0681 against 0.0935, and four of five models show the
larger shift on the arm whose text names no direction at all.

This is not evidence that direction-naming does nothing. It is an
*observational* contrast in which arm is confounded with everything else that
differs between the three walks -- coverage, mechanism-text length, and the
size of the bias being corrected. `time_agnostic_t` has the largest coverage
and the largest hidden bias, so it has the most room to move. The reading to
take is the weak one: the historical data cannot separate process description
from direction statement, in either direction, which is exactly the gap G1's
factorial exists to close.

### Which benchmark configuration is authoritative

`config/benchmark_v21.yaml` is authoritative. The decision was made by
diffing them rather than by preference, and the diff is narrow: the two files
differ in **exactly one key**, `presets.v2.evaluation`. Data generation, the
walk plan, the seed, and the `smoke`, `full` and `v2_smoke` presets are
byte-identical in both.

`benchmark_v21.yaml` adds `lifetime_mean_over_T` to the evaluation targets,
prediction targets, and the strategy-blind, leave-one-block-out and sim2real
blocks, and drops `extra_trees:patterns` from `headline_only_pairs`. The
frozen target hierarchy in `docs/TARGET_EVALUATION_FREEZE.md` requires lifetime
as a robustness target, so v21 is the file consistent with the freeze and the
older one silently omits a target the hierarchy asks for.

Two consequences worth stating plainly. First, because the difference is
confined to `evaluation`, this choice **changes no sample, no walk, no case and
no generated artifact**, and it does not touch the G2 main run at all -- that
runs from `config/final_run_g2.yaml`. Second, the real hazard was never the
file, it was the default: `src/evaluate_benchmark.py` is the *only* consumer of
the `evaluation` block, and it defaulted to the file without the lifetime
target. A resolution recorded in prose while the default kept pointing the
other way would have changed nothing, so the default now points at
`benchmark_v21.yaml`. `src/build_benchmark_data.py` copies the block into a
manifest sidecar without acting on it; every other script reads sections that
are identical in both files, so their defaults are left alone.

## Analysis specification, added 2026-09-01

Fixed before any result was read. The Step 1 slice was running at this point
and its numbers had deliberately not been interpreted; nothing below was chosen
in response to an observed effect.

### (a) `Delta_i` is measured against the naive plug-in, not against `hidden`

For every condition, including `hidden`, the shift is

    Delta_i(condition) = rho2_model(condition) - rho2_naive_i

rather than a difference from `hidden`. Three things follow. `hidden` acquires
a `Delta_i` of its own, so "does the model move at all without a mechanism
description" becomes a measured quantity rather than a definitional zero. The
analytical estimators land on the same axis, so `occ_mle`, `mask_mle` and the
supervised reference can be drawn as reference lines against the model
conditions. And the main figure becomes one plot: the analytical slope as a
line, the per-condition model slopes beside it.

The paired `mechanism - hidden` contrast is kept as well. The two answer
different questions -- one asks where a condition sits, the other how much the
description moved that particular case -- and neither replaces the other.

### (b) The pooled slope is a three-level contrast, and is reported as one

`delta_i` is not a continuous predictor on this panel. It takes three values by
arm: about +0.28 to +0.30 on the three walks, +0.002 on
`node_panel_full_history`, -0.16 on `event_sample_then_full_history`. A pooled
regression of `Delta_i` on `delta_i` is therefore driven almost entirely by the
between-arm contrast, and calling it a slope over a continuous predictor would
overstate what it is.

Accordingly: report the pooled slope, and state in the same sentence that it is
a three-level contrast on the required correction direction. Report **within-arm
slopes separately, per arm**. If the within-arm slopes are near zero, the honest
finding is sign classification without magnitude estimation -- the model gets
the direction right but does not size the correction -- and that is a result to
be named, not a null to be buried.

**Addendum, 2026-09-02.** The paragraph above stands as written; it was fixed
blind and is not edited here. What follows is what has since been measured
against it.

Observed on the **Step 1 two-arm slice only** (`time_agnostic_t` and
`event_sample_then_full_history`, 64 cases): the pooled slope of 0.826
decomposes into a between-arm-means slope of 0.836 and a slope with each arm
centred on its own mean of 0.810.

The clause was right about variance: 62.2% of the predictor's variance is
between-arm, so a pooled regression *is* dominated by the arm contrast in the
sense it meant. What it did not anticipate is that the between-arm and
within-arm slopes come out almost identical. That is the stronger statement, and
it is the one to make: the effect is not an aggregation artefact of the arm
level, because the same relation holds inside an arm as across arms. It is not
the statement that the within-arm component is the whole slope.

Two limits on that addendum, both binding:

- It comes from two arms. The between-arm fit runs through two points, so its
  R^2 of 1 is arithmetic. The five-arm panel adds `node_panel_full_history`,
  where Var(delta) collapses to 0.0026, and the split will not look the same
  there. **Nothing is generalized from this slice**; the decomposition is
  recomputed on all five arms before it is used in any claim.
- Prespecified now, before those numbers exist: on
  `node_panel_full_history` the within-arm slope is **not interpreted as a point
  estimate at all**. Var(true)/Var(delta) is 21.3 on that arm, which makes the
  ratio numerically near-undefined rather than merely imprecise. The placebo
  arm's role in the argument runs through the signed error and the skill score,
  which do not divide by a vanishing predictor variance. Fixing this in advance
  costs nothing; deciding it after seeing the estimate would look like picking.

Reference points for "near zero" depend on which `Delta_i` is in use, and the
two nulls of (i) do not agree. On the paired contrast N0 is zero but N1 is not;
per-arm values are in `results_summary/g3/null_baseline_slopes.csv`, and the
pooled permutation reference is in `results_summary/g3/null_permutation.csv`.

Use a cluster bootstrap over graph groups for the pooled statistic and report
the number of groups. Do **not** additionally cluster on arm: five clusters is
too few for the bootstrap to be reliable.

### (c) Degenerate-output rate

Per model and condition, the share of answers whose `rho_k2` reproduces the
naive plug-in value exactly or to within rounding. This measures directly
whether the model is doing anything with the sample beyond reading a number off
it, and it costs nothing to compute.

### (d) The direction test runs on `rho_3` as well as `rho_2`

Same analysis, second component. The answers already contain it; this is a
post-hoc computation on existing data, not a new run. Report whether the
conclusion holds across k, because a direction effect that appears only on the
headline component is a weaker claim than one that holds along the profile.

### (e) Ceiling contingency for the interaction

"Is the explicit direction needed" is an interaction in the 2x2, and
interactions need roughly four times the N of a main effect. If the direction
hit rate under `mechanism` alone exceeds about 90%, `mechanism_direction` sits
at ceiling on the sign metric and the interaction is unmeasurable there.

Prespecified: in that case the interaction question moves to the magnitude
metrics -- the within-arm slope and the magnitude ratio -- where headroom
remains. The switch is decided from the measured hit rate, not by inspection of
the interaction itself.

### (f) Model roles: which numbers carry a claim

`qwen3.6-27b_think` and `qwen3.6-27b_nothink` are the **confirmatory** models:
version-pinned weights, recorded sampling parameters, fully reproducible on the
cluster. Every headline number is readable off them.

Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` is **exploratory**: its harness injects instructions that
are not part of the frozen prompt and cannot be version-pinned, so it is a
product screen. It is the strongest reader in the suite and worth running, but
a Codex-only finding carries that label wherever it appears. This is stated in
the results text, not deferred to an appendix caveat.

### (g) What the cells are

`hidden` is the (no mechanism described, no direction stated) cell of the 2x2.
It is a cell of the factorial, not a separate baseline.

`metadata_only` is **outside** the 2x2 entirely, because it carries no sample
at all. It is the "metadata plus learned prior" anchor and does not belong in
any cell count.

A reader counting conditions will otherwise see six and either infer six cells
or double-count `hidden`; the factorial has four.

### (h) Dispersion across generations against coverage, added 2026-09-02

**No new runs.** Everything below is computed from answers already being
collected.

#### The claim being tested

Multiple imputation is *proper* when the uncertainty it reports scales with the
information that is missing. A model asked the same question about a
poorly-covered sample should be less repeatable than one asked about a
well-covered sample. If its spread is flat in coverage, its uncertainty is a
stylistic constant rather than a response to the data.

The suite already operationalizes this once, through the stated interval:
`lo90`/`hi90` width and its empirical coverage. This adds the second
operationalization — the **realized** spread across independent generations of
the identical prompt. The two are reported side by side and explicitly named as
two readings of one criterion, because they can disagree, and the disagreement
is itself the result: a model whose stated intervals widen with missing
information while its actual answers do not is calibrated in rhetoric only.

#### Construction

For each (case, condition, model), across the model's generations of that same
prompt, compute the dispersion of the `rho_k2` prediction: **sd** and **IQR**.
Both, not one — with three draws they behave differently under an outlier and
saying which was chosen after seeing them would be the whole trick.

Regress dispersion on the case's dyad `coverage`, **pooled and per arm**, with a
cluster bootstrap over the twelve graph groups — the same resampling unit as
the primary endpoint, for the same reason: cases inside a group share a graph.

The readout is the sign and interval of the slope. Properness predicts
dispersion rising as coverage falls, i.e. a **negative** slope. The primary
statement is the bootstrap interval, not a p-value.

`coverage` spans roughly 0.005 to 0.42 and is heavily right-skewed, so the
regressor is **log10(coverage)**, fixed here rather than chosen from a fit.

#### Which models can carry this at all

Dispersion needs repeated generations, and only Qwen has them:

| model | generations | usable |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.6-27B thinking | 3 | yes |
| Qwen3.6-27B non-thinking | 3 | yes |
| Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` | 1 | **no** |

Thinking and non-thinking are separate models here, not one model in two
settings: they differ in sampling regime and in truncation behaviour, and
pooling them would mix two dispersion processes.

Codex runs one generation, which is the allocation the quota supports, so it
contributes no dispersion estimate. The single exception is the 128 Step 1
prompts, which have two Codex generations. Two draws give a defined sd, no
usable IQR, and an estimate so noisy that it belongs in the text as an order of
magnitude, never in the regression.

#### The threat this analysis has to survive

The regressor is coverage. The dropout is also coverage.

Roughly 44 prompts never terminate at any token budget, and truncation was
already shown to be coverage-dependent — on arm B the truncated cases had
median coverage 0.062 against 0.251 for the rest. A case is only usable here if
*all* of its generations completed, so the cases lost to dropout are
preferentially the low-coverage ones, which is exactly the end of the range the
slope is estimated from. Left unstated this could manufacture the result or
hide it, and which of the two is not predictable in advance.

Therefore, prespecified:

- report `n` cases entering each cell, against the cell's full size;
- report the coverage distribution of included against excluded cases, so the
  reader sees the size of the selection rather than being asked to trust it;
- compute the slope twice, on cases with all three generations complete and on
  cases with at least two, and report both. If they disagree, the disagreement
  is the finding and neither is promoted.

Two further confounds, fixed here rather than discovered later:

- `rho_k2` is a probability, so dispersion is compressed near 0 and 1. If
  coverage correlates with the predicted level, a mean-variance relation alone
  produces a coverage slope. Report the raw slope and one adjusted for the
  case's mean predicted `rho_k2`, and treat a slope that survives only
  unadjusted as unsupported.
- Dispersion measured from three draws is a noisy outcome. Noise in the outcome
  does not bias the slope, it widens the interval — which is a reason not to
  read a null as evidence of flatness, and to say so where the null appears.

#### Separating response dispersion from sample dispersion

Spread across generations of one prompt is response noise: the sample is
identical. The seed-replication subset — 8 graphs x 5 arms x 2 conditions x 3
extra seed slots — varies the sample at fixed condition, so within that subset
the total spread across (seed slot x generation) decomposes into a within-seed
component (response) and a between-seed component (sample).

Report both components and their ratio, per arm. It answers a question the
pooled dispersion cannot: whether a model that spreads more at low coverage is
reacting to the thinness of *its* sample, or whether low-coverage samples
simply differ more from each other. The subset covers 2 of 6 conditions and 8
of 32 graphs, so it qualifies the decomposition and does not replace the main
regression.

#### Blinding, stated exactly

This is added on 2026-09-02, later than (a)-(g), and it is not blind in the way
those were. What had been read by then: the Step 1 slope, direction hit rate,
magnitude ratio, signed `rho_2` bias by condition, and the response and validity
rates; the Qwen completion and truncation rates; and one Codex reproducibility
figure of about 0.07 across the two Step 1 generations, which is the closest
thing to a dispersion number already seen and is why it is named here rather
than left in a code comment.

No dispersion-against-coverage slope has been computed for any model. The
endpoint, the regressor, the estimator and the dropout rule above are fixed
before the first fit.

### (i) Degenerate reference for the slope, added 2026-09-02, revised same day

**This is not a change of metric.** `ProfileMAE` is untouched, and the slope of
`Delta_i` on `delta_i` remains the prespecified test exactly as (a) and (b)
define it. What is added is a validity check *of that test*: every reported
slope is accompanied by the slope a model that has stopped reading the sample
would have produced, so no number is read against an assumed zero.

#### Two degenerate models, and only one of them is at zero

There are two ways to stop reading the sample, and they do not have the same
null.

**N0, no movement.** `P_hidden = P_mechanism`. Whatever the common value is --
the plug-in, a memorized prior, anything -- the paired contrast is
`Delta_i = 0` for every case, and the slope is exactly zero.

**N1, prior fallback.** `P_hidden = naive_i`, `P_mechanism = c`. Then

    Delta_i = c - naive_i = (c - true_i) + delta_i
    slope   = 1 + Cov(delta, c - true) / Var(delta)
            = 1 - Cov(true, delta) / Var(delta)

**N1 does not sit at zero under the paired contrast.** The reason is that
`naive_i` appears inside both `delta_i` and `Delta_i`: the predictor and the
response share a term, and the regression inherits it. Only N0, where both legs
move together and `naive_i` cancels, collapses to zero.

N1 is not a straw man. It is the strategy "the text tells me this sample is
biased, so I distrust it and answer something generic" -- precisely what a model
trained to read a bias warning as a cue to back off would do. It is also the
empirically live option rather than a hypothetical, because the `hidden` leg is
measurably pinned to the plug-in (below), not free.

The constant `c` cancels out of the slope; it only moves the intercept. So the
N1 reference does not depend on which constant a model emits, and a pooled-mean
and an arm-specific constant give the same slope by construction.

#### Measured values

Analytic, from `rho_W5_k2` and `est__plugin_rho_k2` on the 160 factorial cases;
`src/report_null_baselines.py` to `results_summary/g3/null_baseline_slopes.csv`.
Numeric and closed-form columns agree to machine precision.

| scope | N0 | N1 | perfect corrector |
|---|---|---|---|
| pooled | 0.000 | **+0.664** | 1.000 |
| `event_sample_then_full_history` | 0.000 | **+1.588** | 1.000 |
| `node_panel_full_history` | 0.000 | **+1.134** | 1.000 |
| `recent_history_k20` | 0.000 | **-0.209** | 1.000 |
| `time_agnostic_t` | 0.000 | **+0.210** | 1.000 |
| `time_respecting` | 0.000 | **-0.113** | 1.000 |

N1 changes sign across arms. An arm whose slope is read against zero when its
N1 reference is -0.21, or against zero when it is +1.59, is being read against
the wrong line in opposite directions.

#### Why the hidden leg is pinned, and what that settles

Measured correlation between the `hidden` prediction and the naive plug-in,
`results_summary/g3/null_hidden_prior_correlation.csv`:

| model | corr(hidden, naive), range across the five arms |
|---|---|
| Qwen3.6-27B thinking | 0.960 – 0.988 |
| Qwen3.6-27B non-thinking | 0.812 – 0.995 |
| Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` | 0.953 – 1.000 |

Codex reaches 1.000 on `node_panel_full_history` and `time_respecting`, its
prediction's standard deviation matching the plug-in's to six digits: it is
computing the plug-in, not emitting a prior.

This is what makes N1 rather than N0 the relevant null. The hidden leg is not
free to move with the mechanism leg, so a model that backs off *only* under
`mechanism` produces N1, not N0. N0 remains in the table as the reference for
the case where both legs move together.

#### N1 is rejected on this data, and by what margin

Step 1 slice, 64 cases, real plug-in column:

| | slope | R^2 | RMSE | skill score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| observed (`mechanism - hidden`) | +0.826 | 0.797 | 0.117 | **+0.721** |
| N1 prior fallback | +0.883 | 0.543 | 0.228 | **0.000** |
| N0 no movement | 0.000 | — | 0.000 | — |

The slopes are close, which is exactly why the slope alone cannot separate them.
`R^2`, RMSE and the skill score do: N1 scores exactly zero by construction,
because its prediction *is* the best constant, and the observed +0.721 is not
reachable by any model that has stopped reading the sample.

The skill score is therefore not an optional extra. It is the statistic that
discriminates N1 from a real correction, and it is reported per arm, per
condition, per model, as `1 - MSE(model vs rho2_true) / MSE(best constant vs
rho2_true)`, with the best constant taken as the in-sample mean of the truth
over the same scope.

Corroborating the same point from the other side: on that slice the `hidden`
condition scores -0.533 and the plug-in itself -0.554, again nearly the same
number.

#### Permutation reference: schema, fixed

The pooled permutation null is a reported reference line, so its construction is
fixed here rather than implied by code.

- **Permuted:** the predictor, `delta_i`. Never the response.
- **Strata:** the arm. Permuting within arm destroys the within-case pairing and
  leaves each arm's marginal distribution and mean exactly where they were.
- **Response:** the **observed** `Delta_i`. A synthetic response is not
  substituted. An earlier version of this module used the perfect corrector on
  the five-arm panel and reported 0.559; that number answers a different
  question and is withdrawn.
- **Draws:** 4,000, seeded with the master seed 20260901.
- **Reported:** the mean as the reference line, with the standard deviation and
  the 2.5/97.5 percentiles as the band that defines "near it".

On the Step 1 slice: mean **+0.519**, sd 0.051, 95% band [0.420, 0.619]
(`results_summary/g3/null_permutation.csv`).

It is not zero because what survives permutation is the between-arm structure.
The closed form is

    slope_between x Var_between / (Var_between + Var_within)

which on that slice is 0.836 x 0.6218 = **+0.520**, against +0.519 resampled.
A reader can check the reference line without rerunning anything.

#### What is reported from now on

1. Every slope, pooled and per arm, printed beside **both** nulls for the same
   scope, N0 and N1, labelled.
2. `R^2` and residual RMSE beside every slope. On the position framing a
   constant predictor gives R^2 = Var(delta)/(Var(delta) + Var(true)), measured
   at 0.376 pooled and 0.056 to 0.534 per arm.
3. The skill score, per arm, per condition, per model.
4. `metadata_only` is the empirical N1 -- it carries no sample at all -- and is
   reported *against* these baselines rather than described on its own.
5. A robustness row beside every preregistered interval: wild cluster bootstrap
   with Rademacher weights, the same percentile bootstrap over graph instances
   rather than groups, and leave-one-group-out. The preregistered variant --
   percentile over the twelve graph groups -- remains the reported one and is
   not replaced by any of them. Method and first measurement in
   `docs/BOOTSTRAP_ROBUSTNESS_2026-09-02.md`.

#### What this does not license

The nulls are reference lines, not corrections. No slope is divided by,
differenced against, or rescaled by its null. N1 is the worst case a
sample-ignoring model can reach, not the expected value of a null result, and
neither is it to be collapsed into a claim that the null is zero.

#### Variance, which is what makes N1 unstable

`results_summary/g3/null_variance_decomposition.csv`. Pooled, Var(true) 0.0532
against Var(delta) 0.0629. Per arm the truth's variance is identical by
construction, but Var(delta) collapses to 0.0026 on `node_panel_full_history`, a
ratio of 21.3. See (b): the within-arm slope on that arm is prespecified as not
interpretable as a point estimate.

### (j) Arms stratified by channel structure, added 2026-09-02

Prespecified before any Qwen outcome is read. It follows from the channel
decomposition in `docs/BIAS_CHANNELS_2026-09-02.md`, which is analytic and used
no model output.

#### The strata

**Clean arms** — one channel, or none.

| arm | composition | required correction |
|---|---|---|
| `event_sample_then_full_history` | 100% selection | up (+0.163) |
| `time_agnostic_t` | 97% censoring | down (−0.280) |
| `node_panel_full_history` | neither channel bites | none (−0.002) |

**Opposed arms** — two channels pulling against each other.

| arm | selection | censoring | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| `recent_history_k20` | +0.161 | −0.439 | −0.278 |
| `time_respecting` | +0.197 | −0.498 | −0.301 |

#### Why this is two different tasks

On a clean arm the sign of the required correction follows from the mechanism
description by qualitative reasoning: whole-entity sampling with complete
histories over-represents active pairs, so correct downward; a walk that sees
one timestamp per traversal under-counts windows, so correct upward.

On an opposed arm the sign is a **net** quantity. Selection pushes one way,
censoring the other and harder. A competent statistician given only the
mechanism text, and not the magnitudes, could not derive the net sign. The
opposed arms therefore test something the clean arms do not: whether the
capability extends to cases where the direction itself requires weighing two
effects.

#### What is claimed where

1. **The primary claim runs on the clean arms.** Pooled and per arm, over
   `event_sample_then_full_history`, `time_agnostic_t` and
   `node_panel_full_history`.
2. **The opposed arms are reported as an extension**, testing whether the
   ability carries into cases needing a quantitative trade-off.
3. **A failure on the opposed arms is not a failure of mechanism reasoning.**
   It is a failure of quantitative weighing, and is reported in those words.
   The converse is also fixed: success there is a *stronger* result than
   success on the clean arms and is reported as such, not merged into a pooled
   average that hides which arms carried it.

This is a reporting stratification, not a change of estimand. Every arm is still
run, still analysed, still shown. Nothing is dropped.

#### Budget robustness of the net sign — scoped, not yet run

If a moderate budget change flips the net sign on an opposed arm, that belongs
in the text as a limitation, because the "required direction" those cases test
against would not be a stable property of the arm.

This cannot be read off existing files. The G0d budget ladder covers only the
two whole-entity arms; the walks were fixed at 800 steps and the raw walk logs
of the final run were not retained. It is nonetheless cheap and analytic,
because `walk_seed` and `walk_rng_seed` are in the frozen case table and
`src/walks.py` builds a sweep from prefixes of one log: re-running a seed
reproduces the identical walk, and every shorter budget is a prefix of it.

Prespecified procedure: reproduce the 800-step walk from the frozen seeds into a
scratch path, **verify it reproduces the frozen case row exactly** before
trusting anything shorter, then read the channel split at 400, 600 and 800. The
frozen artifacts are not touched and no walk is regenerated in place. Reported
as a sign-stability row per opposed arm, whatever it shows.

#### Twin analysis, prespecified

`time_agnostic_t` and `time_respecting` require nearly the same correction,
−0.280 and −0.301, from very different channel compositions: 97% censoring
against 35% censoring with an opposing selection term. They are a matched pair
in the required answer and a contrast in the stated mechanism.

- If a model shifts nearly equally on both, it is responding to the **implied
  correction**.
- If it shifts differently, it is responding to **text surface**, since the
  correct answer is nearly identical.

Reported as the paired difference of `Delta_i` between the two arms on the same
instance, with the cluster bootstrap over graph groups, alongside the
difference in their required corrections as the reference. It is free from data
already collected.

It is **not run yet**. Executing it means reading the `mechanism` leg for Qwen,
which the blinding note in (i) defers until the decomposition, the
mechanism-space figure and the bootstrap robustness rows are finished. The
specification is fixed here so that ordering costs nothing.
