**The retrospective answer runs opposite to the obvious guess, and that is why
the randomized split is needed.** If naming the direction carried the
disclosure effect, the two direction-naming arms would move more. They move
less: median shift 0.0681 against 0.0935, and four of five models show the
larger shift on the arm whose text names no direction at all.

This is not evidence that direction-naming does nothing. It is an
*observational* contrast in which arm is confounded with everything else that
differs between the three walks -- coverage, mechanism-text length, and the
size of the bias being corrected. `time_agnostic_t` has the largest coverage
and the largest hidden bias, so it has the most room to move. The reading to
take is the weak one: the historical data cannot separate process description
from direction statement, in either direction, which is exactly the gap G1's
factorial exists to close.

### Which benchmark configuration is authoritative

`config/benchmark_v21.yaml` is authoritative. The decision was made by
diffing them rather than by preference, and the diff is narrow: the two files
differ in **exactly one key**, `presets.v2.evaluation`. Data generation, the
walk plan, the seed, and the `smoke`, `full` and `v2_smoke` presets are
byte-identical in both.

`benchmark_v21.yaml` adds `lifetime_mean_over_T` to the evaluation targets,
prediction targets, and the strategy-blind, leave-one-block-out and sim2real
blocks, and drops `extra_trees:patterns` from `headline_only_pairs`. The
frozen target hierarchy in `docs/TARGET_EVALUATION_FREEZE.md` requires lifetime
as a robustness target, so v21 is the file consistent with the freeze and the
older one silently omits a target the hierarchy asks for.

Two consequences worth stating plainly. First, because the difference is
confined to `evaluation`, this choice **changes no sample, no walk, no case and
no generated artifact**, and it does not touch the G2 main run at all -- that
runs from `config/final_run_g2.yaml`. Second, the real hazard was never the
file, it was the default: `src/evaluate_benchmark.py` is the *only* consumer of
the `evaluation` block, and it defaulted to the file without the lifetime
target. A resolution recorded in prose while the default kept pointing the
other way would have changed nothing, so the default now points at
`benchmark_v21.yaml`. `src/build_benchmark_data.py` copies the block into a
manifest sidecar without acting on it; every other script reads sections that
are identical in both files, so their defaults are left alone.

## Analysis specification, added 2026-09-01

Fixed before any result was read. The Step 1 slice was running at this point
and its numbers had deliberately not been interpreted; nothing below was chosen
in response to an observed effect.

### (a) `Delta_i` is measured against the naive plug-in, not against `hidden`

For every condition, including `hidden`, the shift is

    Delta_i(condition) = rho2_model(condition) - rho2_naive_i

rather than a difference from `hidden`. Three things follow. `hidden` acquires
a `Delta_i` of its own, so "does the model move at all without a mechanism
description" becomes a measured quantity rather than a definitional zero. The
analytical estimators land on the same axis, so `occ_mle`, `mask_mle` and the
supervised reference can be drawn as reference lines against the model
conditions. And the main figure becomes one plot: the analytical slope as a
line, the per-condition model slopes beside it.

The paired `mechanism - hidden` contrast is kept as well. The two answer
different questions -- one asks where a condition sits, the other how much the
description moved that particular case -- and neither replaces the other.

### (b) The pooled slope is a three-level contrast, and is reported as one

`delta_i` is not a continuous predictor on this panel. It takes three values by
arm: about +0.28 to +0.30 on the three walks, +0.002 on
`node_panel_full_history`, -0.16 on `event_sample_then_full_history`. A pooled
regression of `Delta_i` on `delta_i` is therefore driven almost entirely by the
between-arm contrast, and calling it a slope over a continuous predictor would
overstate what it is.

Accordingly: report the pooled slope, and state in the same sentence that it is
a three-level contrast on the required correction direction. Report **within-arm
slopes separately, per arm**.

**Revised 2026-09-02, on two counts.**

First, the assertion above that a pooled regression is "driven almost entirely
by the between-arm contrast" is not what the data show. Decomposing the Step 1
pooled slope of 0.826 gives a between-arm-means slope of 0.836 and a slope with
each arm centred on its own mean of 0.810, the latter with R^2 0.588. The
within-arm component is very nearly the whole slope, not a remainder. The
caution was written before any result and was too pessimistic; it stands as a
reason to report both components, not as a description of what they are. (Step 1
has two arms, so the between-arm fit runs through two points and its R^2 of 1 is
arithmetic, not evidence. The five-arm version replaces it.)

Second, "near zero" needs a stated reference, and the reference depends on which
`Delta_i` the slope is built from -- the two definitions in (a) do not share a
null.

- On the **paired** contrast, `mechanism - hidden`, the within-arm null is
  genuinely zero. A constant predictor returns the same value in both
  conditions, so `Delta_i` is identically zero; so does a predictor that
  reproduces the plug-in. What is *not* zero is the sampling spread: permuting
  `delta_i` within an arm over 32 cases gives a 95% null band of roughly
  +/-0.34. A within-arm slope of 0.2 is inside that band and is not evidence of
  magnitude estimation.
- On the **position** axis, `rho2_model(condition) - rho2_naive_i`, the null is
  not zero and is not even the same sign across arms. A constant predictor gives
  +1.59 on `event_sample_then_full_history`, +1.13 on
  `node_panel_full_history`, +0.21 on `time_agnostic_t`, -0.11 on
  `time_respecting`, -0.21 on `recent_history_k20`. Every position-axis slope is
  reported against its own arm's null, never against zero.

So the honest finding of sign classification without magnitude estimation is
read off the paired contrast against its permutation band, and off the position
axis against the arm-specific null slope in
`results_summary/g3/null_baseline_slopes.csv`. It remains a result to be named,
not a null to be buried.

Use a cluster bootstrap over graph groups for the pooled statistic and report
the number of groups. Do **not** additionally cluster on arm: five clusters is
too few for the bootstrap to be reliable.

### (c) Degenerate-output rate

Per model and condition, the share of answers whose `rho_k2` reproduces the
naive plug-in value exactly or to within rounding. This measures directly
whether the model is doing anything with the sample beyond reading a number off
it, and it costs nothing to compute.

### (d) The direction test runs on `rho_3` as well as `rho_2`

Same analysis, second component. The answers already contain it; this is a
post-hoc computation on existing data, not a new run. Report whether the
conclusion holds across k, because a direction effect that appears only on the
headline component is a weaker claim than one that holds along the profile.

### (e) Ceiling contingency for the interaction

"Is the explicit direction needed" is an interaction in the 2x2, and
interactions need roughly four times the N of a main effect. If the direction
hit rate under `mechanism` alone exceeds about 90%, `mechanism_direction` sits
at ceiling on the sign metric and the interaction is unmeasurable there.

Prespecified: in that case the interaction question moves to the magnitude
metrics -- the within-arm slope and the magnitude ratio -- where headroom
remains. The switch is decided from the measured hit rate, not by inspection of
the interaction itself.

### (f) Model roles: which numbers carry a claim

`qwen3.6-27b_think` and `qwen3.6-27b_nothink` are the **confirmatory** models:
version-pinned weights, recorded sampling parameters, fully reproducible on the
cluster. Every headline number is readable off them.

Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` is **exploratory**: its harness injects instructions that
are not part of the frozen prompt and cannot be version-pinned, so it is a
product screen. It is the strongest reader in the suite and worth running, but
a Codex-only finding carries that label wherever it appears. This is stated in
the results text, not deferred to an appendix caveat.

### (g) What the cells are

`hidden` is the (no mechanism described, no direction stated) cell of the 2x2.
It is a cell of the factorial, not a separate baseline.

`metadata_only` is **outside** the 2x2 entirely, because it carries no sample
at all. It is the "metadata plus learned prior" anchor and does not belong in
any cell count.

A reader counting conditions will otherwise see six and either infer six cells
or double-count `hidden`; the factorial has four.

### (h) Dispersion across generations against coverage, added 2026-09-02

**No new runs.** Everything below is computed from answers already being
collected.

#### The claim being tested

Multiple imputation is *proper* when the uncertainty it reports scales with the
information that is missing. A model asked the same question about a
poorly-covered sample should be less repeatable than one asked about a
well-covered sample. If its spread is flat in coverage, its uncertainty is a
stylistic constant rather than a response to the data.

The suite already operationalizes this once, through the stated interval:
`lo90`/`hi90` width and its empirical coverage. This adds the second
operationalization — the **realized** spread across independent generations of
the identical prompt. The two are reported side by side and explicitly named as
two readings of one criterion, because they can disagree, and the disagreement
is itself the result: a model whose stated intervals widen with missing
information while its actual answers do not is calibrated in rhetoric only.

#### Construction

For each (case, condition, model), across the model's generations of that same
prompt, compute the dispersion of the `rho_k2` prediction: **sd** and **IQR**.
Both, not one — with three draws they behave differently under an outlier and
saying which was chosen after seeing them would be the whole trick.

Regress dispersion on the case's dyad `coverage`, **pooled and per arm**, with a
cluster bootstrap over the twelve graph groups — the same resampling unit as
the primary endpoint, for the same reason: cases inside a group share a graph.

The readout is the sign and interval of the slope. Properness predicts
dispersion rising as coverage falls, i.e. a **negative** slope. The primary
statement is the bootstrap interval, not a p-value.

`coverage` spans roughly 0.005 to 0.42 and is heavily right-skewed, so the
regressor is **log10(coverage)**, fixed here rather than chosen from a fit.

#### Which models can carry this at all

Dispersion needs repeated generations, and only Qwen has them:

| model | generations | usable |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.6-27B thinking | 3 | yes |
| Qwen3.6-27B non-thinking | 3 | yes |
| Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` | 1 | **no** |

Thinking and non-thinking are separate models here, not one model in two
settings: they differ in sampling regime and in truncation behaviour, and
pooling them would mix two dispersion processes.

Codex runs one generation, which is the allocation the quota supports, so it
contributes no dispersion estimate. The single exception is the 128 Step 1
prompts, which have two Codex generations. Two draws give a defined sd, no
usable IQR, and an estimate so noisy that it belongs in the text as an order of
magnitude, never in the regression.

#### The threat this analysis has to survive

The regressor is coverage. The dropout is also coverage.

Roughly 44 prompts never terminate at any token budget, and truncation was
already shown to be coverage-dependent — on arm B the truncated cases had
median coverage 0.062 against 0.251 for the rest. A case is only usable here if
*all* of its generations completed, so the cases lost to dropout are
preferentially the low-coverage ones, which is exactly the end of the range the
slope is estimated from. Left unstated this could manufacture the result or
hide it, and which of the two is not predictable in advance.

Therefore, prespecified:

- report `n` cases entering each cell, against the cell's full size;
- report the coverage distribution of included against excluded cases, so the
  reader sees the size of the selection rather than being asked to trust it;
- compute the slope twice, on cases with all three generations complete and on
  cases with at least two, and report both. If they disagree, the disagreement
  is the finding and neither is promoted.

Two further confounds, fixed here rather than discovered later:

- `rho_k2` is a probability, so dispersion is compressed near 0 and 1. If
  coverage correlates with the predicted level, a mean-variance relation alone
  produces a coverage slope. Report the raw slope and one adjusted for the
  case's mean predicted `rho_k2`, and treat a slope that survives only
  unadjusted as unsupported.
- Dispersion measured from three draws is a noisy outcome. Noise in the outcome
  does not bias the slope, it widens the interval — which is a reason not to
  read a null as evidence of flatness, and to say so where the null appears.

#### Separating response dispersion from sample dispersion

Spread across generations of one prompt is response noise: the sample is
identical. The seed-replication subset — 8 graphs x 5 arms x 2 conditions x 3
extra seed slots — varies the sample at fixed condition, so within that subset
the total spread across (seed slot x generation) decomposes into a within-seed
component (response) and a between-seed component (sample).

Report both components and their ratio, per arm. It answers a question the
pooled dispersion cannot: whether a model that spreads more at low coverage is
reacting to the thinness of *its* sample, or whether low-coverage samples
simply differ more from each other. The subset covers 2 of 6 conditions and 8
of 32 graphs, so it qualifies the decomposition and does not replace the main
regression.

#### Blinding, stated exactly

This is added on 2026-09-02, later than (a)-(g), and it is not blind in the way
those were. What had been read by then: the Step 1 slope, direction hit rate,
magnitude ratio, signed `rho_2` bias by condition, and the response and validity
rates; the Qwen completion and truncation rates; and one Codex reproducibility
figure of about 0.07 across the two Step 1 generations, which is the closest
thing to a dispersion number already seen and is why it is named here rather
than left in a code comment.

No dispersion-against-coverage slope has been computed for any model. The
endpoint, the regressor, the estimator and the dropout rule above are fixed
before the first fit.

### (i) Degenerate reference for the slope, added 2026-09-02

**This is not a change of metric.** `ProfileMAE` is untouched, and the slope of
`Delta_i` on `delta_i` remains the prespecified test exactly as (a) and (b)
define it. What is added is a validity check *of that test*: every reported
slope is accompanied by the slope a model that does not read the sample would
have produced, so no number is read against an assumed zero.

#### The algebra, and where it applies

Under the **position** definition, `Delta_i = rho2_model - rho2_naive_i`, a
constant predictor `P_i = c` gives

    slope = [Var(naive) - Cov(naive, true)] / Var(delta)
          = 1 + Cov(naive - true, true) / Var(delta)

which sits near 1 whenever the naive bias is roughly uncorrelated with the
truth. The constant `c` cancels: it is absorbed into the intercept. So the
degenerate reference does not depend on *which* constant a model emits, and
`constant_pooled` and `constant_arm` are the same slope by construction. On
this panel they also share an intercept, because the truth is a property of the
instance and all five arms see the same 32 instances.

Under the **paired** definition, `Delta_i = rho2_model(mechanism) -
rho2_model(hidden)`, the same constant gives `c - c = 0` for every case. The
slope is exactly zero and there is no variance left to explain.

Both definitions are in use — (a) keeps both deliberately — so both nulls are
reported, labelled by definition.

a pooled fit still recovers the between-arm structure. Any pooled slope is read

The **plug-in reproducer** is the degenerate model this panel actually contains.
Measured correlation between the `hidden` prediction and the naive plug-in, in

|---|---|

| Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` | 0.953 – 1.000 |

Codex reaches 1.000 on `node_panel_full_history` and `time_respecting`, with the
standard deviation of its predictions matching the plug-in's to six digits: it
is computing the plug-in, not emitting a prior. So the constant-output model
that produces the artefact is not a description of any model in this suite, and
the empirically relevant degenerate reference is the plug-in reproducer, whose
slope is 0 on both definitions. The constant column is retained as the
worst-case bound, not as the expected null.

#### Variance, which is what makes the reference unstable

`results_summary/g3/null_variance_decomposition.csv`. Pooled, Var(true) 0.0532
against Var(delta) 0.0629. Per arm the truth's variance is identical by
construction, but Var(delta) collapses: 0.0026 on `node_panel_full_history`, a
ratio of 21.3. That arm is the near-zero-correction placebo, so its degenerate
reference is both the largest and the least stable, and a slope reported there
without its null would be the easiest number in the suite to over-read.

#### What is reported from now on

1. Every slope, pooled and per arm, is printed beside its null-model slope for
   the same definition and scope.
2. `R^2` and residual RMSE accompany every slope. They are the discriminator the
   slope alone does not provide: a perfect corrector gives R^2 near 1, while a
   constant predictor on the position axis gives R^2 = Var(delta)/(Var(delta) +
   Var(true)) — measured at 0.376 pooled, and between 0.056 and 0.534 per arm.
3. A **skill score**, `1 - MSE(model vs rho2_true) / MSE(best constant vs
   rho2_true)`, per arm, per condition, per model. It never differences against
   the naive baseline, so the artefact cannot enter it: a constant predictor
   scores 0 by construction and a model has to beat the best achievable
   constant to score above it. The best constant is the in-sample mean of the
   truth over the same scope, which is the most generous denominator available.
4. `metadata_only` is the empirical constant predictor — it carries no sample at
   all — and is reported *against* these baselines rather than described on its
   own. If its slope lands near the constant-predictor null while the sampled
   conditions do not, that is the cleanest available demonstration that the
   sampled conditions are reading the sample.

#### What this does not license

The null columns are a reference, not a correction. No slope is divided by,
differenced against, or rescaled by its null. The prespecified test stays as
specified; the null is printed next to it so the reader can judge it.
